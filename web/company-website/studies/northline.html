02 — Northline

Audiences you can explain.

Audience intelligence

A representative study, composed to show the shape of an engagement.

The situation

Northline’s cheapest subscribers were arriving through lookalike buys nobody could describe. The cost looked efficient. Partnerships stalled because sales could say what the click cost, and could not say who had arrived.

The work

We built an audience picture from what people actually read: the section, the depth, and the cadence of return. Four groups replaced the lookalike. Each group fit on a page a seller could leave with a partner — who they are, what they finish, what they decline.

An audience you cannot brief is a receipt.

The media team kept buying, but the buy had a name. When a group stopped reading, it left the plan instead of being “optimized” into a blur.

What remained

Cost per qualified subscription fell 27%. The sales team stopped apologizing for the audience. They could name it, and a partner could recognize the reader in the description.

What the team kept

  • Four audiences with a name and a reading habit
  • A one-page brief for each group
  • A weekly check for when a group goes quiet
